# Activity 4 — Copilot in Excel

> **Synthetic data only:** All names, figures, events, and operational details in this activity are fictional and do not represent Louis Dreyfus Company operations.

## Purpose
Use natural-language questions to understand fictional APAC operations data, identify variances and exceptions, and create an executive-ready summary.

**Suggested time:** 10–12 minutes  
**Required file:** `Synthetic APAC Operations Data.xlsx`

## Before Copilot
You would inspect columns, apply filters, create formulas, look for outliers, build charts, and translate the results into a business explanation.

## With Copilot
You can ask questions in everyday language, receive formula suggestions, identify data-quality issues, find patterns, create visuals, and generate a first executive summary.

## Why it is useful
Copilot makes analysis more approachable while keeping the supporting workbook available. You can move faster from rows of data to questions and insights—but must still validate formulas, assumptions, and conclusions.

## Setup
1. Open `Synthetic APAC Operations Data.xlsx` in Excel from OneDrive or SharePoint.
2. Confirm that the data is formatted as an Excel table.
3. Open Copilot in Excel.

## Step-by-step prompts

### 1. Understand the workbook
> Summarize this workbook in 30 seconds. Explain what the APAC operations data contains and identify the most important columns.

Then ask:

> What missing values, duplicates, inconsistent labels, or unusual values should I review?

### 2. Analyze performance
> Compare planned and actual volume. Identify the regions or business areas with the largest negative variance and explain the business impact based only on the data.

Then ask:

> Identify the most important trends and exceptions in this table. Group the findings into Positive Signals, Watch Items, and Immediate Attention.

### 3. Add a calculated column
> Propose a new column called Attention Category. Classify each row as On Track, Monitor, or Needs Attention using status, delay days, and the variance between planned and actual volume. Explain the logic and formula before applying it.

Review the proposed thresholds, formula, and categories before applying them.

### 4. Create a visual and summary
> Create a chart comparing planned and actual volume by region or business area. Use a clear title and an executive-friendly layout.

Then ask:

> Create a short executive summary with three data-supported insights and three recommended actions. Clearly separate facts from recommendations.

### Optional challenge
> Analyze the historical pattern and estimate the likely direction for the next reporting period. Explain the assumptions and limitations of the analysis.

## Participant reflection and adoption question
- Did Copilot reveal an exception or pattern that was easy to overlook? What still required your judgment?
- Which workbook do you regularly open and then spend time figuring out what changed or what needs attention?

## Key takeaway
Copilot in Excel helps users understand unfamiliar data, improve data quality, create formulas and visuals, and move from rows of information to business insights and recommended actions.

## Verify before using the output
Check table ranges, filters, units, formulas, thresholds, totals, chart labels, and every quoted figure. Confirm that conclusions follow from the data and that forecasts or recommendations state their assumptions and limitations.
