# Activity 1 — Copilot in Teams

> **Use your own approved work content:** Complete this activity privately using a suitable Teams meeting, chat, or channel discussion. Do not display or share confidential, personal, HR, legal, medical, or otherwise sensitive information with the group.

## Purpose
Use Copilot to catch up on a real conversation, identify what was decided, and understand what needs your attention without manually reviewing the entire discussion.

**Suggested time:** 10–12 minutes  
**Required material:** One of your own recent Teams meeting recaps or transcribed meetings, or a substantial chat or channel discussion.

## Before Copilot
You might scroll through messages, replay parts of a recording, copy notes elsewhere, and manually separate decisions and actions from general discussion.

## With Copilot
You can ask targeted questions, create a structured recap, identify unresolved issues, and prepare a follow-up while keeping the source conversation available for checking.

## Why it is useful
Copilot is not limited to a generic summary. You can continue asking what changed, where viewpoints differed, what remains unresolved, and what you personally need to do next.

## Choose suitable content

Select a conversation that:

- Contains enough discussion to summarize
- Includes updates, questions, decisions, or actions
- Is relevant to your normal work
- Is appropriate to review privately during the training session

If possible, choose a meeting you attended recently but have not yet fully reviewed.

## Setup

1. Open your chosen Teams meeting recap, transcript, chat, or channel discussion.
2. Open Copilot for the conversation.
3. Keep the source content available so you can validate the answers.
4. Work privately. You do not need to share your screen or the Copilot response.

## Step-by-step prompts

### 1. Catch up quickly

> Summarize this discussion. Organize the summary under Key Updates, Decisions, Risks, and Action Items.

Then ask:

> What are the most important points I should know before responding or taking action?

### 2. Find decisions and actions

> List the decisions made and the action items. Include owners and due dates only when they were explicitly mentioned.

Then ask:

> Which questions, risks, or issues remain unresolved?

### 3. Explore the discussion

> What changed during the discussion, and were different viewpoints expressed? Base the answer only on the available conversation or transcript.

Then make the result personal:

> Based on this discussion, what actions or follow-ups appear relevant to me? Do not invent responsibilities that were not stated.

### 4. Prepare a follow-up

> Draft a concise follow-up message for the participants. Confirm the decisions, list the next actions, and politely highlight unresolved items. Do not invent owners, dates, or commitments.

Review the draft. Posting the message is not required for this exercise.

### Optional live-meeting prompts

During a transcribed meeting, try:

> What did I miss?

> What viewpoints have been shared about the current issue?

> Suggest two questions I can ask to help the group reach a decision.

## Participant reflection and adoption question

- Did Copilot surface anything that was easy to overlook?
- What manual review did it reduce, and what still required your judgment?
- Which recurring meeting, channel, or group chat would you most like to catch up on faster?

## Key takeaway
Copilot in Teams helps people catch up faster, identify decisions and actions, and participate more effectively without manually reviewing an entire conversation or transcript.

## Verify before using the output
Check every name, figure, date, decision, owner, and due date against the source conversation. Confirm that unresolved issues have not been presented as decisions. Copilot provides a starting point, not automatic approval.
