# Louis Dreyfus Company — Microsoft 365 Copilot Session Setup and File Map

## Session goal
Experience how Microsoft 365 Copilot can help with real everyday work in Teams and Outlook, then use a connected synthetic APAC operations scenario to practise content creation and analysis in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint.

> **Responsible use:** For Teams and Outlook, learners should use their own approved work content and complete the activities privately. Choose content that is appropriate to review during a training session. Do not display or share confidential, personal, HR, legal, medical, or otherwise sensitive information with the group.

> **Synthetic training data:** The Word, Excel, and PowerPoint activities use fictional names, figures, events, locations, dates, and operational details. They do not represent Louis Dreyfus Company operations.

## Recommended run order

| Order | Activity | Suggested time | Outcome |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Teams Copilot | 10–12 min | Catch up on one of your own meetings, chats, or channel discussions and identify decisions, risks, and actions. |
| 2 | Outlook Copilot | 10–12 min | Understand one of your own email threads and prepare a clear draft response. |
| 3 | Word Copilot | 10–12 min | Turn the supplied synthetic weekly notes into a structured one-page operations brief. |
| 4 | Excel Copilot | 10–12 min | Analyze the supplied synthetic operations data for variances, trends, and exceptions. |
| 5 | PowerPoint Copilot | 10–12 min | Convert the synthetic brief and workbook insights into a leadership presentation. |

Allow an additional 5–10 minutes for setup and final reflection.

## How the activities connect

Teams and Outlook are personal-use exercises based on each learner’s own approved work content. Learners do not need to share their source information or Copilot results with the group.

Word, Excel, and PowerPoint remain a connected synthetic workflow:

1. Structure the supplied weekly operations notes in Word.
2. Analyze the supporting operations workbook in Excel.
3. Use the resulting brief and workbook to create a PowerPoint leadership update.

The Word, Excel, and PowerPoint activities are not affected by the change to Teams and Outlook.

## Learner preparation

Before the session, ask each learner to identify:

- A recent Teams meeting with a recap or transcript, or a substantial chat/channel discussion
- A suitable email conversation with several replies, a request, update, or decision to understand

The selected items should be relevant enough to demonstrate value but appropriate to review privately during training. Learners should not be required to share their screens, source content, or generated output.

## Facilitator preparation

1. Confirm participant access to Microsoft 365 Copilot in Teams, Outlook, Word, Excel, and PowerPoint.
2. Ask learners in advance to identify a suitable Teams conversation and Outlook email thread.
3. Explain that Teams and Outlook activities are completed privately using approved work content.
4. Place the synthetic Word files and Excel workbook in OneDrive or SharePoint, with permissions tested in advance.
5. Confirm the Excel data is formatted as a table and that Copilot can access it.
6. Keep the Markdown and JSON synthetic files available as facilitator reference copies for checking generated outputs.
7. Share the participant guides and explain the recommended run order.
8. Remind participants that Copilot output is a starting point, not automatic approval.

## Adoption method for every activity

1. **Before:** Identify how the task is normally completed and where effort is spent.
2. **Try:** Run the prompts and review the result.
3. **Compare:** Notice what Copilot accelerated, improved, or surfaced.
4. **Verify:** Check facts, figures, sources, tone, assumptions, owners, and dates.
5. **Commit:** Name one repeatable task where the technique could be used next week.

> **Core message:** Copilot shifts effort from searching, summarizing, formatting, and starting from scratch to reviewing, deciding, and improving.

## Final reflection

- Which activity removed the most repetitive work?
- Where did Copilot improve quality rather than only speed?
- What still required human judgment?
- Which prompt or technique will you use in your next working week?
- How will you choose appropriate work content while protecting sensitive information?
