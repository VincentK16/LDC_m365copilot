# Activity 2 — Copilot in Outlook

> **Use your own approved work content:** Complete this activity privately using a suitable email thread. Do not display or share confidential, personal, HR, legal, medical, or otherwise sensitive information with the group.

## Purpose
Use Copilot to understand a real email conversation and prepare a clear, audience-appropriate draft response.

**Suggested time:** 10–12 minutes  
**Required material:** One of your own suitable email threads containing several replies, a request, an update, a question, or a decision.

## Before Copilot
You might read every reply, reconstruct who is asking for what, identify the latest position, and compose a response from a blank page.

## With Copilot
You can summarize the thread, identify the request and missing information, generate a response, adjust it for the audience, and use coaching to improve clarity and tone.

## Why it is useful
Copilot combines understanding and communication in one workflow. You spend less effort reconstructing the thread and more effort making the right decision and checking the final response.

## Choose a suitable email thread

Select a thread that:

- Contains several messages or replies
- Has a clear request, update, question, or next step
- Is relevant to your normal work
- Is appropriate to review privately during the training session

Avoid highly sensitive conversations. Do not use personal, HR, legal, medical, disciplinary, or confidential commercial content for the exercise.

## Setup

1. Open your selected email thread in Outlook.
2. Review the participants and subject to confirm you selected the intended conversation.
3. Work privately. You do not need to share your screen or the Copilot response.
4. Plan to save a draft only. Sending an email is not part of this exercise.

## Step-by-step prompts

### 1. Summarize the thread

Use **Summary by Copilot**, or ask:

> Summarize this email thread in five bullets. Highlight the request, current status, concerns, decisions, and next actions.

Then ask:

> What information is missing or unclear before I can respond or make a final decision?

### 2. Identify what matters to you

> What am I being asked to do, and what response or action appears to be expected from me? Base the answer only on this email thread.

Then ask:

> Are there conflicting statements, unanswered questions, or commitments that need clarification?

### 3. Draft a response

Select **Reply**, open Copilot, and enter:

> Draft a professional reply that acknowledges the request, confirms only the agreed facts and actions, asks for missing information, and proposes a clear next step. Do not invent facts, dates, owners, or commitments.

### 4. Adjust the message

Try one or more refinements:

> Make the message shorter and more direct.

> Rewrite this for a senior leadership audience.

> Make the tone collaborative and confident.

> Clearly separate confirmed information from items that still need clarification.

### 5. Use Coaching by Copilot

Use **Coaching by Copilot** to review tone, clarity, and reader impact. Apply only the suggestions that make the message more accurate and useful.

Save the response as a draft or discard it after the exercise. Do not send it unless you independently decide to do so after completing all normal checks.

## Participant reflection and adoption question

- Did Copilot make the request and expected action clearer?
- Did it identify missing information or conflicting points that were easy to overlook?
- What still required human judgment?
- Which type of email takes the most time for you to understand or respond to?

## Key takeaway
Copilot in Outlook reduces the time needed to understand lengthy threads and helps create clear, audience-appropriate responses while keeping the user in control of the final message.

## Verify before using the output
Check recipients, names, figures, dates, decisions, commitments, requested information, and tone against the complete thread. Remove unsupported claims and confirm the draft is suitable for its audience. Sending is always your decision.
